Pied Beauty

by Gerard Manley Hopkins

Written 1877 Published 1918 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Written in 1877 and published posthumously in 1918, this 'curtal sonnet' — a compressed ten-and-a-half-line form Hopkins invented — praises God for the beauty of all things dappled, spotted, brindled, and variable, and for the unchanging creator who made all this changeable beauty.

Quick Answer

"Pied Beauty" is a poem by Gerard Manley Hopkins, written in 1877 and published posthumously in 1918. In a compressed sonnet form Hopkins invented himself, the speaker praises God for the beauty of everything variegated, dappled, and mottled in nature — skies like a brindled cow, rose-moles on trout, finches' wings, landscape divided by trades — and concludes by praising the unchanging God who created all this beautiful variety.

Glory be to God for dappled things—
  For skies of couple-colour as a brinded cow;
    For rose-moles all in stipple upon trout that swim;
Fresh-firecoal chestnut-falls; finches' wings;
  Landscape plotted and pieced—fold, fallow, and plough;
    And all trades, their gear and tackle and trim.

All things counter, original, spare, strange;
  Whatever is fickle, freckled (who knows how?)
    With swift, slow; sweet, sour; adazzle, dim;
He fathers-forth whose beauty is past change:
                        Praise him.

Public domain. Published posthumously 1918.

Analysis

Form and Structure

A 'curtal sonnet' — Hopkins's compressed version of the Petrarchan sonnet, consisting of six lines, four and a half lines, and a closing half-line, in sprung rhythm (Hopkins's own invented meter, based on the number of stressed syllables per line rather than a fixed pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables).

Themes

The glory of variety, contrast, and dappled things as evidence of divine creativity. Hopkins catalogues contrasts — swift/slow, sweet/sour, adazzle/dim — as expressions of God's creative abundance. The paradox at the heart of the poem is that the ever-changing, multiform beauty of the natural world points to an unchanging ('past change') God who sustains all of it.

Historical Context

Hopkins, a Jesuit priest, wrote the poem in 1877 during his theological training and sent it to his friend Robert Bridges, but it was not published until 1918, twenty-nine years after his death. Hopkins's sprung rhythm and compression were revolutionary for their time and had enormous influence on twentieth-century poetry.

About Gerard Manley Hopkins

Gerard Manley Hopkins was born on July 28, 1844, in Stratford, Essex, England, and became one of the most innovative poets of the Victorian era, though almost none of his work was published during his lifetime. He converted to Catholicism in 1866 and was ordained as a Jesuit priest in 1877, the year he wrote some of his finest poems. Hopkins invented 'sprung rhythm,' a metrical system based on stressed syllables rather than a fixed pattern, and his poetry's intense verbal compression and sound effects were a major influence on twentieth-century verse. He died in Dublin in 1889.
