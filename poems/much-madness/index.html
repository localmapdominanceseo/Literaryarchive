Much Madness Is Divinest Sense

by Emily Dickinson

Written 1862 Published 1890 American Romanticism Public Domain

Written around 1862 and published posthumously in 1890, this eight-line poem argues that the majority is wrong: much madness is the divinest sense to discerning eyes, while much sense is the starkest madness. Assent and you are sane; demur and you are dangerous and handled with a chain.

Quick Answer

"Much Madness Is Divinest Sense" is a poem by Emily Dickinson, written around 1862. Much madness is the divinest sense to discerning eyes; much sense the starkest madness. The majority prevails. Assent, and you are sane. Demur — you are straightway dangerous, and handled with a chain.

Much Madness is divinest Sense—
To a discerning Eye—
Much Sense—the starkest Madness—
'Tis the Majority
In this, as all, prevail—
Assent—and you are sane—
Demur—you're straightway dangerous—
And handled with a Chain—

Analysis

Form and Structure

Eight lines in common meter with slant rhyme, one of Dickinson's most compressed political poems.

Themes

Social conformity and the violent suppression of nonconformity. The poem defines sanity and madness as social constructs enforced by majority rule, with the threat of chains for those who demur.

Historical Context

Published posthumously in 1890. One of Dickinson's most socially critical poems.

About Emily Dickinson

Emily Dickinson was born on December 10, 1830, in Amherst, Massachusetts, and spent nearly her entire life there. Though she wrote nearly 1,800 poems, fewer than a dozen were published during her lifetime. Her work, marked by unconventional capitalization, slant rhyme, and dashes, was largely discovered and published after her death in 1886. She is now considered one of the most important and original voices in American poetry, known for her meditations on death, immortality, nature, and the inner life.
