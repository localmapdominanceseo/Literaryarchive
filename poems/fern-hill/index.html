Fern Hill

by Dylan Thomas

Written 1945 Published 1945 American Modernism Full text withheld — rights review

Published in 1945, this poem recalls the ecstasy of childhood summers at his aunt's farm in Wales — time held the speaker green and dying, though he sang in his chains like the sea.

Quick Answer

"Fern Hill" is a poem by Dylan Thomas, published in 1945. It recalls the joy and freedom of childhood summers on a Welsh farm, with the speaker young and easy under the apple boughs, green and carefree. The poem moves from this joy to the revelation at the end: time held him green and dying, and though he sang in his chains like the sea, he was always already in time's grip.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Six nine-line stanzas in loose syllabic verse, Thomas's characteristic richly textured sound with heavy alliteration and assonance.

Themes

The paradise of childhood and its inevitable loss — not through any single event but through the simple fact that time was always already passing even in the moments of greatest joy. The closing 'chains' are both time's constraint and the beauty of the poetry itself.

Historical Context

Published in Deaths and Entrances (1946). One of Thomas's most celebrated poems, drawing on his childhood summers at his aunt Ann Jones's farm in Carmarthenshire, Wales.

About Dylan Thomas

Dylan Thomas was born on October 27, 1914, in Swansea, Wales, and became one of the most celebrated poets of the twentieth century, known for his rich, densely textured verse and his powerful voice in readings and recordings. His debut collection 18 Poems (1934) caused an immediate sensation when he was just nineteen. His villanelle 'Do Not Go Gentle into That Good Night' is among the most quoted poems in the English language. He died in New York in 1953 at the age of thirty-nine.
