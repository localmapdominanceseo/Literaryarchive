To the Forgotten Dead

by A.E. Housman

Written 1895 Published 1896 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Published in A Shropshire Lad (1896), this poem is a terse salute to the dead who lived and died before the current generation and whose names are lost — they were good fellows once.

Quick Answer

Here Dead We Lie is a poem by A.E. Housman. Here dead we lie because we did not choose to live and shame the land from which we sprung. Life, to be sure, is nothing much to lose, but young men think it is and we were young.

Here dead we lie because we did not choose
To live and shame the land from which we sprung.
Life, to be sure, is nothing much to lose;
But young men think it is, and we were young.

Public domain. Published 1922 in Last Poems.

Analysis

Form and Structure

One four-line stanza — an epitaph in four lines.

Themes

The sacrifice of young men who died for their country and the dry, unsentimental acknowledgment that life is not much to lose, but young men think it is.

Historical Context

Published in Last Poems (1922).

About A.E. Housman

Alfred Edward Housman was born on March 26, 1859, in Fockbury, Worcestershire, England, and became both one of the greatest classical scholars and one of the most beloved English poets of his era. A Shropshire Lad (1896), published at his own expense after being rejected by publishers, became one of the most popular poetry collections ever published in English. He died in Cambridge in 1936.
