Mutability

by Percy Bysshe Shelley

Written 1814 Published 1816 English Romanticism Public Domain

Published in 1816 in Alastor and Other Poems, this short philosophical lyric argues that we are clouds, we are leaves, we are waves — nothing rests but changes, and even the name of change is forgotten.

Quick Answer

"Mutability" is a short poem by Shelley, published in 1816. We are clouds that veil the midnight moon; we are leaves before the storm; we are waves that rise and fall. Man's yesterday may ne'er be like his morrow. Nothing in life may last or stay except the change itself.

We are as clouds that veil the midnight moon;
How restlessly they speed, and gleam, and quiver,
Streaking the darkness radiantly!—yet soon
Night closes round, and they are lost for ever:

Or like forgotten lyres, whose dissonant strings
Give various response to each varying blast,
To whose frail frame no second motion brings
One mood or modulation like the last.

We rest.—A dream has power to poison sleep;
We rise.—One wandering thought pollutes the day;
We feel, conceive or reason, laugh or weep;
Embrace fond woe, or cast our cares away:

It is the same!—For, be it joy or sorrow,
The path of its departure still is free:
Man's yesterday may ne'er be like his morrow;
Nought may endure but Mutability.

Public domain. Published 1816 in Alastor and Other Poems.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Two stanzas of eight lines each in alternating iambic tetrameter and trimeter, the light form containing a philosophically serious argument about impermanence.

Themes

The Buddhist-like argument that nothing persists — every state of mind, every experience, every configuration is transient. The consolation (if any) is that this applies equally to suffering.

Historical Context

Published in Alastor and Other Poems (1816). One of Shelley's most purely philosophical short poems.

About Percy Bysshe Shelley

Percy Bysshe Shelley was born on August 4, 1792, in Horsham, England, and became one of the major poets of English Romanticism despite controversy during his lifetime over his radical political and religious views. His poetry combines lyrical intensity with sustained engagement with politics, philosophy, and the natural world. He drowned in a boating accident off the coast of Italy in 1822, at the age of twenty-nine.
