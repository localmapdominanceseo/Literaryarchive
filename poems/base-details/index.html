Base Details

by Siegfried Sassoon

Written 1917 Published 1918 World War I Poetry Public Domain

Written in 1917 and published in 1918 in Counter-Attack and Other Poems, this bitter six-line satirical poem is spoken in the voice of a fat, scarlet Major who sends young men to their deaths from the safety of the base, then goes home to die in bed.

Quick Answer

"Base Details" is a short satirical poem by Siegfried Sassoon, published in 1918. Spoken in the persona of a comfortable, fat, scarlet Major safely behind the lines, it imagines how he would handle the war: speed glum heroes up the line to death, while dining and drinking safely at the base, then toddle home and die in bed.

If I were fierce, and bald, and short of breath,
I'd live with scarlet Majors at the Base,
And speed glum heroes up the line to death.
You'd see me with my puffy petulant face,
Guzzling and gulping in the best hotel,
Reading the Roll of Honour. 'Poor young chap,'
I'd say—'I used to know his father well;
Yes, we've lost heavily in this last scrap.'
And when the war is done and youth stone dead,
I'd toddle safely home and die—in bed.

Public domain. Published 1918 in Counter-Attack and Other Poems.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Six lines of loose iambic pentameter in rhyming couplets, with the brevity and compression of an epigram. The persona poem format lets Sassoon inhabit and expose the Major's voice without comment.

Themes

The class-based hypocrisy of military command in World War I — senior officers living comfortably while sending working-class soldiers to die. The contrast between 'glum heroes' going 'up the line to death' and the Major who will 'die in bed' is the poem's entire content and entire indictment.

Historical Context

Sassoon published 'Base Details' in Counter-Attack and Other Poems (1918), a collection written during his public protest against the continuation of the war and his subsequent treatment at Craiglockhart War Hospital, where he met Wilfred Owen. It is one of his most compressed and savage anti-war satires.

About Siegfried Sassoon

Siegfried Sassoon was born on September 8, 1886, in Brenchley, Kent, England, and became one of the most important poets of World War I, known for bitter, satirical poems attacking the war's conduct and the complacency of those who sent young men to die. He served on the Western Front, won the Military Cross, and then publicly refused to return to duty in 1917 — a protest that was handled by sending him to Craiglockhart War Hospital, where he met Wilfred Owen and shaped his poetry. He died in 1967.
