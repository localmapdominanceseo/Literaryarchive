Hurrahing in Harvest

by Gerard Manley Hopkins

Written 1877 Published 1918 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Written in September 1877 and published posthumously in 1918, this sonnet describes the harvest-time sky above the Clwyd Valley and Hopkins's sudden awareness that the wind, the hills, the clouds all express Christ's presence — his heart leaps up.

Quick Answer

Hurrahing in Harvest is a poem by Hopkins, published posthumously in 1918. Summer ends now; barbarous in beauty, the stooks arise around. Meal-drift moulded ever and melted across skies. The azurous hung hills are his world-wielding shoulder majestic — what a heart-throe, birth of a brain, what finds of rapture — the heart rears wings bold and bolder and hurls for him, O half hurls earth for him off under his feet.

Summer ends now; now, barbarous in beauty, the stooks arise
Around; up above, what wind-walks! what lovely behaviour
Of silk-sack clouds! has wilder, wilful-wavier
Meal-drift moulded ever and melted across skies?

I walk, I lift up, I lift up heart, eyes,
Down all that glory in the heavens to glean our Saviour;
And, éyes, heárt, what looks, what lips yet gave you a
Rapturous love's greeting of realer, of rounder replies?

And the azurous hung hills are his world-wielding shoulder
Majestic—as a stallion stalwart, very-violet-sweet!—
These things, these things were here and but the beholder
Wanting; which two when they once meet,
The heart rears wings bold and bolder
And hurls for him, O half hurls earth for him off under his feet.

Public domain. Written 1877, published posthumously 1918.

Analysis

Form and Structure

A Petrarchan sonnet in sprung rhythm.

Themes

The experience of God's presence through the harvest landscape — the hills and clouds as Christ's body, the moment of recognition as an ecstatic transport.

Historical Context

Written September 1, 1877. Hopkins described it as the outcome of half an hour of extreme enthusiasm. Published posthumously in 1918.

About Gerard Manley Hopkins

Gerard Manley Hopkins was born on July 28, 1844, in Stratford, Essex, England, and became one of the most innovative poets of the Victorian era, though almost none of his work was published during his lifetime. He converted to Catholicism in 1866 and was ordained as a Jesuit priest in 1877, the year he wrote some of his finest poems. Hopkins invented 'sprung rhythm,' a metrical system based on stressed syllables rather than a fixed pattern, and his poetry's intense verbal compression and sound effects were a major influence on twentieth-century verse. He died in Dublin in 1889.
