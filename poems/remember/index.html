Remember

by Christina Rossetti

Written 1849 Published 1862 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Written in 1849 when Rossetti was only nineteen and published in 1862 in her collection Goblin Market and Other Poems, this sonnet is spoken by someone anticipating her own death, asking her beloved to remember her, before ultimately releasing him from that obligation.

Quick Answer

"Remember" is a sonnet by Christina Rossetti, written in 1849 and published in 1862. The speaker asks her lover to remember her after she has died, but then reverses course, telling him that it is better he forget and be happy than remember and be sad, distinguishing between memory and grief.

Remember me when I am gone away,
Gone far away into the silent land;
When you can no more hold me by the hand,
Nor I half turn to go yet turning stay.
Remember me when no more day by day
You tell me of our future that you plann'd:
Only remember me; you understand
It will be late to counsel then or pray.
Yet if you should forget me for a while
And afterwards remember, do not grieve:
For if the darkness and corruption leave
A vestige of the thoughts that once I had,
Better by far you should forget and smile
Than that you should remember and be sad.

Analysis

Form and Structure

The poem is a Petrarchan sonnet, fourteen lines divided into an octave and a sestet, with the turn (or volta) arriving in the ninth line, where the speaker shifts from insisting on being remembered to permitting herself to be forgotten.

Themes

The poem explores grief and memory from an unusual angle: rather than the perspective of someone left behind, it is spoken by the person who will die. Its central turn argues that love can mean releasing someone from the burden of permanent grief, prioritizing the living person's happiness over being remembered at all.

Historical Context

Rossetti wrote 'Remember' in 1849 at age nineteen, though it was not published until 1862 in her first major collection, Goblin Market and Other Poems. It has remained one of her most frequently anthologized poems and a touchstone of Victorian poetry about mortality and devotion.

About Christina Rossetti

Christina Rossetti was born on December 5, 1830, in London, the youngest of the four Rossetti children. She is the most admired Victorian woman poet, known for her lyrical grace, her devout Anglican faith, and her poems of love, death, and longing. Her first major collection Goblin Market and Other Poems (1862) was immediately celebrated. Her brother Dante Gabriel Rossetti was a central figure in the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood. She died in London in 1894.
