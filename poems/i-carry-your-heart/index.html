i carry your heart with me

by E.E. Cummings

Written 1952 Published 1952 American Modernism Full text withheld — rights review

Published in 1952 in Complete Poems, this love poem by E.E. Cummings declares that the speaker carries the beloved's heart inside his own — it is the deepest secret of all that exists, the root and sky of all things, the everything of everything.

Quick Answer

"i carry your heart with me" is a love poem by E.E. Cummings, published in 1952. The speaker carries the beloved's heart within his own, goes wherever she goes, fears nothing because she is with him, wants nothing she does not want. Her heart is the deepest secret of all existence — the root of the root, the sky of the sky, the wonder keeping the stars apart.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Four stanzas of varying length in Cummings's characteristic lower-case, unpunctuated style, with parenthetical asides that create an intimate, spoken quality. The form enacts the content: the beloved's heart is held inside parentheses within the speaker's heart.

Themes

Total love as union — carrying another person's heart so completely that it becomes inseparable from your own experience of the world. The poem's resolution, 'it is the wonder that's keeping the stars apart,' elevates love to a cosmic force.

Historical Context

Published in Complete Poems: 1904-1962. Cummings's unconventional typography — no capitals, minimal punctuation — was a deliberate formal choice, not carelessness. Now one of the most frequently quoted love poems in American literature, often read at weddings.

About E.E. Cummings

Edward Estlin Cummings was born on October 14, 1894, in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and became one of the most distinctive American poets of the twentieth century, known for his radical experiments with typography, punctuation, capitalization, and syntax. He published his first major collection, Tulips and Chimneys, in 1923, and continued to innovate formally throughout his career. His love poems, particularly 'i carry your heart with me,' are now among the most frequently quoted in American literature. He died in North Conway, New Hampshire, in 1962.
