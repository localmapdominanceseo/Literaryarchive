Drummer Hodge

by Thomas Hardy

Written 1899 Published 1902 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Published in 1902, this short poem mourns a young English drummer boy buried in South Africa during the Boer War — under alien stars in a strange country, his body becoming part of a foreign soil.

Quick Answer

Drummer Hodge is a poem by Thomas Hardy, published in 1902. A young drummer boy is buried without ceremony in the veldt of South Africa during the Boer War. He will know no foreign sky as home, but the foreign constellations will be his stars, and the foreign soil part of him for ever.

They throw in Drummer Hodge, to rest
Uncoffined—just as found:
His landmark is a kopje-crest
That breaks the veldt around;
And foreign constellations west
Each night above his mound.

Young Hodge the Drummer never knew—
Fresh from his Wessex home—
The meaning of the broad Karoo,
The Bush, the dusty loam,
And why uprose to nightly view
Strange stars amid the gloam.

Yet portion of that unknown plain
Will Hodge for ever be;
His homely Northern breast and brain
Grow to some Southern tree,
And strange-eyed constellations reign
His stars eternally.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Three six-line stanzas of alternating iambic tetrameter and trimeter with ABABAB rhyme.

Themes

The pathos of a young man buried far from home, becoming part of an alien landscape he never understood. Hardy refuses to glamorize the death.

Historical Context

Published in Poems of the Past and the Present (1902). Written during the Second Boer War (1899-1902).

About Thomas Hardy

Thomas Hardy was born on June 2, 1840, in Higher Bockhampton, Dorset, England, and became one of the greatest novelists and poets of the Victorian and Edwardian eras. After Jude the Obscure (1895) was attacked for immorality, he stopped writing fiction and devoted himself entirely to poetry, producing eight volumes. His elegies for his first wife Emma, written after her sudden death in 1912, are considered among the finest in the English language. He died in Dorchester in 1928.
