The Starlight Night

by Gerard Manley Hopkins

Written 1877 Published 1918 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Written in 1877 and published posthumously in 1918, this sonnet urges the reader to look at the stars — to buy the stars with prayer and patience — for they are the barn and the granary of God, and behind them Christ and his mother and all the hallows.

Quick Answer

The Starlight Night is a poem by Hopkins, published posthumously in 1918. Look at the stars! Look, look up at the skies! O look at all the fire-folk sitting in the air! The grey lawns cold where the quaking quicksilver lies! Buy then! bid then! What? Prayer, patience, alms, vows. Look, look: the delicate-quivering silver star behind the barn — this piece-bright paling shuts the spouse Christ home, Christ and his mother and all his hallows.

Look at the stars! look, look up at the skies!
O look at all the fire-folk sitting in the air!
The bright boroughs, the circle-citadels there!
Down in dim woods the diamond delves! the elves'-eyes!
The grey lawns cold where gold, where quickgold lies!
Wind-beat whitebeam! airy abeles set on a flare!
Flake-doves sent floating forth at a farmyard scare!—
Ah well! it is all a purchase, all is a prize.

Buy then! bid then!—What?—Prayer, patience, alms, vows.
Look, look: a May-mess, like on orchard boughs!
Look! March-bloom, like on mealed-with-yellow sallows!
These are indeed the barn; withindoors house
The shocks. This piece-bright paling shuts the spouse
Christ home, Christ and his mother and all his hallows.

Public domain. Written 1877, published posthumously 1918.

Analysis

Form and Structure

A Petrarchan sonnet in iambic pentameter.

Themes

The stars as signs of divine presence and the need to purchase their beauty with spiritual effort — prayer and patience.

Historical Context

Written February 24, 1877, at St Beuno's College. Published posthumously in 1918.

About Gerard Manley Hopkins

Gerard Manley Hopkins was born on July 28, 1844, in Stratford, Essex, England, and became one of the most innovative poets of the Victorian era, though almost none of his work was published during his lifetime. He converted to Catholicism in 1866 and was ordained as a Jesuit priest in 1877, the year he wrote some of his finest poems. Hopkins invented 'sprung rhythm,' a metrical system based on stressed syllables rather than a fixed pattern, and his poetry's intense verbal compression and sound effects were a major influence on twentieth-century verse. He died in Dublin in 1889.
