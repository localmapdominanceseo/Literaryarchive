Wild Nights—Wild Nights!

by Emily Dickinson

Written 1861 Published 1891 American Romanticism Public Domain

Written around 1861 and published posthumously in 1891, this compressed lyric fantasizes about mooring tonight in the beloved's port — done with wind and compass, rowing in Eden.

Quick Answer

"Wild Nights—Wild Nights!" is a poem by Emily Dickinson, written around 1861 and published posthumously in 1891. The speaker cries out for wild nights with the beloved, imagining that with them, luxury would be futile — done with the compass, done with the chart. Rowing in Eden, mooring in the beloved's port tonight.

Wild Nights—Wild Nights!
Were I with thee
Wild Nights should be
Our luxury!

Futile—the winds—
To a Heart in port—
Done with the Compass—
Done with the Chart!

Rowing in Eden—
Ah—the Sea!
Might I but moor—Tonight—
In thee!

Public domain. Text per Academy of American Poets (poets.org).

Analysis

Form and Structure

Three quatrains in common meter with slant rhyme, compressed to the point of explosive lyric energy.

Themes

Erotic longing and the fantasy of complete union with the beloved — the storm outside making the safe harbor of love more desirable. One of Dickinson's most explicitly sensual poems.

Historical Context

Published posthumously in 1891. Thomas Wentworth Higginson hesitated to include it, worried readers would take it as too explicit.

About Emily Dickinson

Emily Dickinson was born on December 10, 1830, in Amherst, Massachusetts, and spent nearly her entire life there. Though she wrote nearly 1,800 poems, fewer than a dozen were published during her lifetime. Her work, marked by unconventional capitalization, slant rhyme, and dashes, was largely discovered and published after her death in 1886. She is now considered one of the most important and original voices in American poetry, known for her meditations on death, immortality, nature, and the inner life.
