Song (When I Am Dead, My Dearest)

by Christina Rossetti

Written 1848 Published 1862 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Written in 1848 and published in 1862 in Goblin Market and Other Poems, this song is spoken by someone anticipating death, gently instructing their beloved not to grieve or plant symbolic flowers over them — they may remember or forget, and the speaker will not know or mind either way.

Quick Answer

"Song" by Christina Rossetti, written in 1848 and published in 1862, is spoken by someone facing death who tells their beloved: don't plant sad flowers over me, don't weep, if you remember, remember; if you forget, it's fine. The speaker will be in a shadowy half-world, perhaps remembering, perhaps forgetting, and will not mind either way whether they are mourned or not.

When I am dead, my dearest,
Sing no sad songs for me;
Plant thou no roses at my head,
Nor shady cypress tree:
Be the green grass above me
With showers and dewdrops wet;
And if thou wilt, remember,
And if thou wilt, forget.

I shall not see the shadows,
I shall not feel the rain;
I shall not hear the nightingale
Sing on, as if in pain:
And dreaming through the twilight
That doth not rise nor set,
Haply I may remember,
And haply may forget.

Public domain. Published 1862 in Goblin Market and Other Poems.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Two eight-line stanzas in alternating iambic tetrameter and trimeter with an ABCBDEFE rhyme scheme, light and musical despite the subject, creating a gentle song-like tone that matches the poem's instruction not to grieve.

Themes

A gentle release of the obligation to mourn, unlike Rossetti's more famous 'Remember,' which asks to be remembered before reversing course. This poem begins with release: plant no sad flowers, shed no tears. The speaker is already at a distance from the world of the living and finds the whole question of grief relatively insignificant.

Historical Context

Rossetti wrote the poem in 1848 at age seventeen or eighteen, the same year she wrote 'Remember.' The two poems represent different sides of the same meditation on death and love — 'Remember' insisting on memory before releasing it, 'Song' releasing the obligation from the start.

About Christina Rossetti

Christina Rossetti was born on December 5, 1830, in London, the youngest of the four Rossetti children. She is the most admired Victorian woman poet, known for her lyrical grace, her devout Anglican faith, and her poems of love, death, and longing. Her first major collection Goblin Market and Other Poems (1862) was immediately celebrated. Her brother Dante Gabriel Rossetti was a central figure in the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood. She died in London in 1894.
