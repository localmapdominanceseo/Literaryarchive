The Agony

by George Herbert

Written 1633 Published 1633 Metaphysical Poetry Public Domain

Published in 1633 in The Temple, this poem argues that two vast, immeasurable spaces — sin and love — can be measured if we look at Christ's passion: sin is what pressed him in Gethsemane, love is the blood that came from his side.

Quick Answer

"The Agony" is a poem by George Herbert, published in 1633. Philosophers measure heaven and earth and seas, but two vast spaces exceed measurement: sin and love. Sin is what Christ was pressed with in the garden of Gethsemane — and love is the blood that flowed from his pierced side, a liquor sweet and most divine. Who knows not love, let him taste that cup.

Philosophers have measur'd mountains,
Fathom'd the depths of seas, of states, and kings,
Walk'd with a staff to heav'n, and traced fountains:
But there are two vast, spacious things,
The which to measure it doth more behove:
Yet few there are that sound them; Sinne and Love.

Who would know Sinne, let him repair
Unto Mount Olivet; there shall he see
A man so wrung with pains, that all his hair,
His skinne, his garments bloudie be.
Sinne is that presse and vice, which forceth pain
To hunt his cruell food through ev'ry vein.

Who knows not Love, let him assay
And taste that juice, which on the crosse a pike
Did set again abroach; then let him say
If ever he did taste the like.
Love is that liquour sweet and most divine,
Which my God feels as bloud; but I, as wine.

Public domain. Published 1633 in The Temple.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Three six-line stanzas of alternating iambic pentameter and tetrameter with ABABCC rhyme, compressed and precise.

Themes

The measurement of immeasurable spiritual realities through the physical events of the Passion — Christ's sweating blood in Gethsemane measures sin's weight; his pierced side measures love's depth.

Historical Context

Published in The Temple (1633). One of Herbert's most compressed theological poems, turning the tools of natural philosophy toward spiritual knowledge.

About George Herbert

George Herbert was born on April 3, 1593, in Montgomery, Wales, and became one of the leading Metaphysical poets, known for devotional verse of striking formal ingenuity. He served as a Church of England priest in his final years and prepared his entire collection, The Temple, for posthumous publication before his death in 1633. His poetry combines intense personal piety with wit and formal experimentation.
