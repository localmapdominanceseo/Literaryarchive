There's a Certain Slant of Light

by Emily Dickinson

Written 1861 Published 1890 American Romanticism Public Domain

Written around 1861 and published posthumously in 1890, this poem describes winter afternoon light that oppresses like cathedral tunes — an internal difference where the meanings are — and when it goes, the landscape listens and shadows hold their breath.

Quick Answer

"There's a Certain Slant of Light" is a poem by Emily Dickinson, written around 1861. Winter afternoon light oppresses like cathedral tunes and leaves a heavenly hurt that gives no scar but internal difference where the meanings are. None may teach it anything. When it comes, the landscape listens; when it goes, the shadows hold their breath.

There's a certain Slant of light,
Winter Afternoons—
That oppresses, like the Heft
Of Cathedral Tunes—

Heavenly Hurt, it gives us—
We can find no scar,
But internal difference—
Where the Meanings, are—

None may teach it—Any—
'Tis the seal Despair—
An imperial affliction
Sent us of the Air—

When it comes, the Landscape listens—
Shadows—hold their breath—
When it goes, 'tis like the Distance
On the look of Death—

Public domain. Text per Academy of American Poets (poets.org).

Analysis

Form and Structure

Four quatrains in common meter with slant rhyme, the poem's formal control containing a genuinely difficult emotional and philosophical perception.

Themes

The depression and existential heaviness associated with winter afternoon light — not a describable or teachable thing but a 'heavenly hurt' that changes everything internally without leaving a visible mark.

Historical Context

Published posthumously in 1890. Considered one of Dickinson's greatest poems and one of the finest short poems on depression and seasonal affect in English literature.

About Emily Dickinson

Emily Dickinson was born on December 10, 1830, in Amherst, Massachusetts, and spent nearly her entire life there. Though she wrote nearly 1,800 poems, fewer than a dozen were published during her lifetime. Her work, marked by unconventional capitalization, slant rhyme, and dashes, was largely discovered and published after her death in 1886. She is now considered one of the most important and original voices in American poetry, known for her meditations on death, immortality, nature, and the inner life.
