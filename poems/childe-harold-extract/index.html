Childe Harold's Pilgrimage (Extract)

by Lord Byron

Written 1818 Published 1818 English Romanticism Public Domain

Published 1818.

Quick Answer

Childe Harold's Pilgrimage (Extract) is a poem published 1818.

There is a pleasure in the pathless woods,
There is a rapture on the lonely shore,
There is society, where none intrudes,
By the deep sea, and music in its roar:
I love not man the less, but Nature more,
From these our interviews, in which I steal
From all I may be, or have been before,
To mingle with the Universe, and feel
What I can ne'er express, yet can not all conceal.

Roll on, thou deep and dark blue Ocean—roll!
Ten thousand fleets sweep over thee in vain;
Man marks the earth with ruin—his control
Stops with the shore;—upon the watery plain
The wrecks are all thy deed, nor doth remain
A shadow of man's ravage, save his own,
When, for a moment, like a drop of rain,
He sinks into thy depths with bubbling groan,
Without a grave, unknell'd, uncoffin'd, and unknown.

About Lord Byron

George Gordon Byron, known as Lord Byron, was born on January 22, 1788, in London, and became one of the most famous and controversial figures of English Romanticism, celebrated equally for his poetry and his scandalous personal life. His work ranges from brief, polished lyrics to the sprawling satirical epic Don Juan, and the brooding, charismatic outsider figure he depicted in his writing gave rise to the term 'the Byronic hero.' He died in Greece in 1824 while supporting the Greek War of Independence.
