The Harbor

by Carl Sandburg

Written 1916 Published 1916 American Modernism Public Domain

Published in 1916 in Chicago Poems, this short poem contrasts a woman standing on a dock, looking out toward the morning mist over the water, with the brutal labor of men loading freight below her — finding beauty and harshness side by side at the harbor.

Quick Answer

"The Harbor" is a poem by Carl Sandburg, published in 1916 in Chicago Poems. A woman stands on a breakwater looking out at the morning mist rising from the blue-black water. Below her, men throw bags of wheat onto freight cars — brutal, sweating labor in contrast to the beauty of the scene above.

Passing through huddled and ugly walls
By doorways where women
Haggard from lack of womanhood
Dream and forget the arrows of the sun,
A dream of white sea-gull wings
Flashing and flying in sunlight and rain
The harbor rests—
A woman stood on a breakwater
Looking with eyes over the blue-black morning water
Toward the sunrise, the red and changing sunrise:
Men sweat and the ships go—
Hundreds and hundreds go—
With bags of wheat.
The woman prays.

Public domain. Published 1916 in Chicago Poems.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Eight lines of free verse, split between the woman's vantage point above and the workers below, with the contrast doing the poem's primary work.

Themes

The coexistence of beauty and brutal labor in industrial urban life — a characteristic Sandburg subject. The woman and the workers occupy the same harbor but experience it completely differently.

Historical Context

Published in Chicago Poems (1916), alongside 'Chicago,' 'Fog,' and other poems of industrial American life.

About Carl Sandburg

Carl Sandburg was born on January 6, 1878, in Galesburg, Illinois, to Swedish immigrant parents, and became one of the most popular American poets of the twentieth century. He worked many jobs — farm worker, soldier, journalist — before establishing himself as a poet with Chicago Poems (1916). He won three Pulitzer Prizes, two for poetry and one for his biography of Abraham Lincoln. He died in Flat Rock, North Carolina, in 1967.
