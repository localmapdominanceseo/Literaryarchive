Composed upon Westminster Bridge

by William Wordsworth

Written 1802 Published 1807 English Romanticism Public Domain

Written on September 3, 1802, while Wordsworth was crossing Westminster Bridge early in the morning, this sonnet describes London in the still of early morning as the most beautiful sight on earth — the city wearing the beauty of morning like a garment.

Quick Answer

"Composed upon Westminster Bridge" is a sonnet by Wordsworth, published in 1807. Earth has not anything to show more fair than the silent city at dawn, wearing the beauty of morning like a garment. All bright and glittering in the smokeless air, ships and towers and domes and temples lie open to the fields and sky. The river glideth at his own sweet will. God! the very houses seem asleep.

Earth has not anything to show more fair:
Dull would he be of soul who could pass by
A sight so touching in its majesty:
This City now doth, like a garment, wear
The beauty of the morning; silent, bare,
Ships, towers, domes, theatres, and temples lie
Open unto the fields, and to the sky;
All bright and glittering in the smokeless air.
Never did sun more beautifully steep
In his first splendour, valley, rock, or hill;
Ne'er saw I, never felt, a calm so deep!
The river glideth at his own sweet will:
Dear God! the very houses seem asleep;
And all that mighty heart is lying still!

Analysis

Form and Structure

A Petrarchan sonnet in iambic pentameter, the poem notable for describing a city scene in a form Wordsworth usually reserved for nature.

Themes

The unexpected beauty of the city at dawn — normally Wordsworth distrusts cities, but here the absence of smoke and human activity reveals a natural beauty in the urban landscape that rivals anything in nature.

Historical Context

Written September 3, 1802, and published in Poems in Two Volumes (1807). One of very few Wordsworth poems set in a city.

About William Wordsworth

William Wordsworth was born on April 7, 1770, in Cockermouth, England. Alongside Samuel Taylor Coleridge, he helped launch the English Romantic movement with the 1798 publication of Lyrical Ballads. Wordsworth's poetry championed plain language, the beauty of the natural world, and the inner life of ordinary people. He served as England's Poet Laureate from 1843 until his death in 1850, and his long autobiographical poem The Prelude is considered one of the central achievements of English Romanticism.
