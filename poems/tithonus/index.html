Tithonus

by Alfred Lord Tennyson

Written 1860 Published 1860 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Published in 1860, Tithonus is a dramatic monologue spoken by the mythological figure Tithonus — the mortal lover of the goddess Aurora who was granted immortality but forgot to ask for eternal youth, and now grows old without being able to die.

Quick Answer

"Tithonus" is a dramatic monologue by Tennyson, published in 1860. Tithonus, mortal lover of the dawn goddess Aurora, was granted immortality but not eternal youth and now ages forever. He begs Aurora to take back her gift and let him die. The happy men that have the power to die — that is what he envies.

The woods decay, the woods decay and fall,
The vapours weep their burthen to the ground,
Man comes and tills the field and lies beneath,
And after many a summer dies the swan.
Me only cruel immortality
Consumes: I wither slowly in thine arms,
Here at the quiet limit of the world,
A white-hair'd shadow roaming like a dream
The ever-silent spaces of the East,
Far-folded mists, and gleaming halls of morn.

Alas! for this gray shadow, once a man—
So glorious in his beauty and thy choice,
Who madest him thy chosen, that he seem'd
To his great heart none other than a God!
I ask'd thee, 'Give me immortality.'
Then didst thou grant mine asking with a smile,
Like wealthy men, who care not how they give.
But thy strong Hours indignant work'd their wills,
And beat me down and marr'd and wasted me,
And tho' they could not end me, left me maim'd
To dwell in presence of immortal youth,
Immortal age beside immortal youth,
And all I was, in ashes.
 Can thy love,
Thy beauty, make amends, tho' even now,
Close over us, the silver star, thy guide,
Shines in those tremulous eyes that fill with tears
To hear me? Let me go: take back thy gift:
Why should a man desire in any way
To vary from the kindly race of men,
Or pass beyond the goal of ordinance
Where all should pause, as is most meet for all?

Analysis

Form and Structure

Seventy-six lines of blank verse, the poem's steady pace enacting Tithonus's endless existence.

Themes

The horror of immortality without youth — a reversal of the usual Romantic longing for eternal life. Tithonus is a warning about the danger of getting what you wished for.

Historical Context

First drafted as 'Tithon' in 1833 and published in revised form in 1860. A companion piece to Ulysses — where Ulysses resolves to keep striving, Tithonus longs for the release that comes to ordinary mortals.

About Alfred Lord Tennyson

Alfred Lord Tennyson was born on August 6, 1809, in Somersby, Lincolnshire, England, and became the dominant poet of the Victorian era, serving as Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom from 1850 until his death in 1892. His work ranges from brief lyrics and dramatic monologues to the epic In Memoriam A.H.H., his long elegy for his close friend Arthur Henry Hallam. He died at Aldworth, Surrey, in 1892.
