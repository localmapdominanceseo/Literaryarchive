Tears, Idle Tears

by Alfred Lord Tennyson

Written 1847 Published 1847 Victorian Poetry Public Domain

Published in 1847 as a song in The Princess, this short lyric meditates on inexplicable tears rising from the depth of some divine despair — tears for what is not, the days that are no more.

Quick Answer

"Tears, Idle Tears" is a lyric by Tennyson, published in 1847. The speaker weeps idle tears and knows not what they mean — tears from the depth of some divine despair, rising in the heart while looking on the happy Autumn-fields. They are for the days that are no more — fresh as the first beam on a sail, sad as the last which reddens over one that sinks.

Tears, idle tears, I know not what they mean,
Tears from the depth of some divine despair
Rise in the heart, and gather to the eyes,
In looking on the happy Autumn-fields,
And thinking of the days that are no more.

Fresh as the first beam glittering on a sail,
That brings our friends up from the underworld,
Sad as the last which reddens over one
That sinks with all we love below the verge;
So sad, so fresh, the days that are no more.

Ahh, sad and strange as in dark summer dawns
The earliest pipe of half-awaken'd birds
To dying ears, when unto dying eyes
The casement slowly grows a glimmering square;
So sad, so strange, the days that are no more.

Dear as remember'd kisses after death,
And sweet as those by hopeless fancy feign'd
On lips that are for others; deep as love,
Deep as first love, and wild with all regret;
O Death in Life, the days that are no more.

Public domain. Published 1847 in The Princess.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Twenty lines of blank verse in four stanzas, each developing a different image for the 'days that are no more.'

Themes

Elegiac grief for the irrecoverable past — the speaker cannot name the loss but only describe its quality through a sequence of images.

Historical Context

Published in The Princess (1847). One of the most discussed of all Tennyson's lyrics for its formal innovation — blank verse lyric — and its psychological depth.

About Alfred Lord Tennyson

Alfred Lord Tennyson was born on August 6, 1809, in Somersby, Lincolnshire, England, and became the dominant poet of the Victorian era, serving as Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom from 1850 until his death in 1892. His work ranges from brief lyrics and dramatic monologues to the epic In Memoriam A.H.H., his long elegy for his close friend Arthur Henry Hallam. He died at Aldworth, Surrey, in 1892.
