So, We'll Go No More a Roving

by Lord Byron

Written 1817 Published 1830 English Romanticism Public Domain

Published 1830.

Quick Answer

So, We'll Go No More a Roving is a poem published 1830.

So, we'll go no more a roving
  So late into the night,
Though the heart be still as loving,
  And the moon be still as bright.

For the sword outwears its sheath,
  And the soul wears out the breast,
And the heart must pause to breathe,
  And love itself have rest.

Though the night was made for loving,
  And the day returns too soon,
Yet we'll go no more a roving
  By the light of the moon.

About Lord Byron

George Gordon Byron, known as Lord Byron, was born on January 22, 1788, in London, and became one of the most famous and controversial figures of English Romanticism, celebrated equally for his poetry and his scandalous personal life. His work ranges from brief, polished lyrics to the sprawling satirical epic Don Juan, and the brooding, charismatic outsider figure he depicted in his writing gave rise to the term 'the Byronic hero.' He died in Greece in 1824 while supporting the Greek War of Independence.
