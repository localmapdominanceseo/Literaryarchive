The Revolt of Mother

by Mary E. Wilkins Freeman

Written 1800 Published 1800 Public Domain

Published in 1890 in Harper's New Monthly Magazine, 'The Revolt of Mother' is Mary E. Wilkins Freeman's most famous story. Sarah Penn has waited forty years for her husband to build her a proper house as promised. When he builds a new barn instead, she moves the family into the barn while he is away.

Themes

A woman's quiet, decisive assertion of her rights against passive male authority — Sarah's revolt is nonviolent and domestic, but it is absolute.

Historical Context

Published in Harper's New Monthly Magazine, September 1890. One of the foundational stories of American women's regional realism.

About Mary E. Wilkins Freeman

Mary Eleanor Wilkins Freeman was born on October 31, 1852, in Randolph, Massachusetts. One of the leading figures of New England regional realism, she wrote stories of fierce, independent women constrained by poverty and social convention. 'The Revolt of Mother' (1890) is her most celebrated story. She died in Metuchen, New Jersey, in 1930.
