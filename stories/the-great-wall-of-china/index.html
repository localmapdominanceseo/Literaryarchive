Before the Law

by Franz Kafka

Written 1800 Published 1800 Public Domain

Published in 1915, 'Before the Law' is Kafka's shortest and most parable-like piece. A man from the country comes to the Law and asks to be admitted. The doorkeeper says he cannot admit him now. The man waits his whole life. As he is dying, he asks why no one else came to this door. The doorkeeper answers: this door was made only for you. Now I shall close it.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Themes

The inaccessibility of justice and authority — the man could have walked through at any moment but was persuaded not to by the mere existence of the doorkeeper. The Law's accessibility is a trap.

Historical Context

Written 1914, published 1915 in Selbstwehr. Later incorporated into The Trial as the priest's parable.

About Franz Kafka

Franz Kafka was born on July 3, 1883, in Prague, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. Writing in German, he produced some of the most disquieting and influential fiction of the twentieth century: 'The Metamorphosis,' 'The Trial,' 'The Castle,' and 'In the Penal Colony.' Most of his work was published posthumously by his friend Max Brod, against Kafka's explicit instructions to burn it. He died of tuberculosis in Kierling, Austria, in 1924.
