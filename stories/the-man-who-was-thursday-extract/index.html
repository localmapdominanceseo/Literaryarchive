The Invisible Man

by G.K. Chesterton

Written 1800 Published 1800 Public Domain

Published in 1911 in The Innocence of Father Brown, 'The Invisible Man' is one of the finest Father Brown mysteries. A woman asks Father Brown to protect her from a man who threatens her. A man is murdered and four witnesses all say no one entered the building. Father Brown reveals the murderer: the postman — invisible because everyone ignores those who deliver things.

Themes

Social invisibility — the story is about the people society ignores: servants, postmen, laborers. Father Brown notices them; the others do not.

Historical Context

Published in The Innocence of Father Brown (1911).

About G.K. Chesterton

Gilbert Keith Chesterton was born on May 29, 1874, in London. A prolific journalist, novelist, and poet, he is best known for his Father Brown detective stories, which combine mystery with Catholic theology. His novel The Man Who Was Thursday (1908) is his most celebrated work of fiction. He died in Beaconsfield in 1936.
