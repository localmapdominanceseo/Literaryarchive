The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas

by Ursula K. Le Guin

Written 1800 Published 1800 Public Domain

Published in 1973 in New Dimensions 3, 'The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas' by Ursula K. Le Guin describes a utopian city of joy and beauty — whose happiness depends on the suffering of one child kept in a basement in filth. Most citizens accept this arrangement once they learn of it. Some, after seeing the child, walk away from Omelas toward an unknown place.

Analysis

Form and Structure

Themes

The utilitarian bargain and its refusal — the story asks whether a society's happiness can be justified by the suffering of one innocent. Most characters accept it; a few refuse to participate.

Historical Context

Published 1973. Won the Hugo Award for Best Short Story in 1974. Inspired by a phrase in William James.

About Ursula K. Le Guin

Ursula K. Le Guin was born on October 21, 1929, in Berkeley, California. One of the greatest writers of speculative fiction, her Earthsea novels, The Left Hand of Darkness (1969), The Dispossessed (1974), and her short stories transformed science fiction and fantasy. She died in Portland, Oregon, in 2018.
